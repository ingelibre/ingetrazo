# Texture sources

- `library/wood/wood_bark.png` (Bark014), `library/wood/wood_bark_olive.png`
  (Bark012), `library/stone/stone_rock.png` (Rock058),
  `library/stone/stone_moss_wall.png` (Rock064),
  `library/stone/stone_river_pebbles.png` (Rocks022),
  `library/ground/grass_lawn.png` (Grass005) and
  `library/floor/paving_concrete.png` (PavingStones128) — colour maps from
  **ambientCG** (ambientcg.com), dedicated to the public domain under
  **CC0 1.0** (no attribution required; noted here for provenance).
  Downscaled to 512×512 for the bundled library; the originals are
  seamless PBR sets at up to 8K.
- `library/water/water_calm.png` — procedural seamless water (sum of
  tileable sine waves + sparkle highlights), original work generated for
  IngeTrazo, GPL-3.0-or-later like the rest of the project.
- Everything else under `library/` and the loose `*.png` swatches are
  generated by `scripts/gen_textures.py` (original work, same licence).

- The 397 textures marked `"src": "sh3t"` in `library.json` come from the
  **Sweet Home 3D** texture libraries, imported with `scripts/import_sh3t.py`.
  All are **CC BY 4.0**, and the attribution the licence asks for travels in
  the manifest (`"author"` per texture) as well as here:
  - **eTeks/Scopia 1.3.1** — 340 textures by **Scopia**
    (https://www.scopia.es), published for Sweet Home 3D by eTeks.
  - **Contributions 1.3.1** — 57 textures by **Ola-Kristian Hoff** (33) and
    **Emmanuel Puybaret** (24).

  What makes them worth importing is not the images but the catalogue: it
  declares each texture's real-world size, which is what the manifest's
  `sw`/`sh` carry. A brick tiled at an arbitrary size reads as a mosaic.
